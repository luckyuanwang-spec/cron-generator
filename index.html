📚 Cron Expression Guide

Field Allowed Values Special Characters
Minute 0-59 * , - /
Hour 0-23 * , - /
Day of Month 1-31 * , - /
Month 1-12 * , - /
Day of Week 0-6 (0=Sunday) * , - /

Special Characters

  • * - Any value
  • , - Value list separator (1,3,5)
  • - - Range of values (1-5)
  • / - Step values (*/15)

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is a cron expression?
A cron expression is a string of 5 fields that defines when a scheduled task should run. Each field represents minute, hour, day of month, month, and day of week.
How do I read a cron expression?
The format is: minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week. For example, "0 9 * * 1-5" means "at 9:00 AM, every day of month, every month, Monday through Friday."
What does the asterisk (*) mean?
An asterisk means "every possible value". So "* * * * *" means "every minute of every hour of every day" - essentially every minute.
What do the day numbers mean?
In cron, 0-6 represent days of the week: 0=Sunday, 1=Monday, 2=Tuesday, 3=Wednesday, 4=Thursday, 5=Friday, 6=Saturday.
How do I run a job every 5 minutes?
Use "*/5 * * * *". The "*/5" means "every 5 units" - in this case, every 5 minutes. You can use any interval like */10, */15, */30.
Can I specify multiple days?
Yes! Use commas for specific days (1,3,5) or hyphens for ranges (1-5). For example, "0 9 * * 1,3,5" runs at 9 AM on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.
